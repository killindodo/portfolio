PERSONAL ETHOS & CONVICTION

Philosophy: The Architecture of Relentless Curiosity

Struggles, breakthroughs, and the stubborn conviction that nothing is a black box if you refuse to look away.

I didn't start with high-end dev workstations or pristine cloud sandboxes. I started in Class 7 on an aging family computer, writing my first lines of HTML in pure wonder that text on a screen could come alive. Later came a beat-up, second-hand Samsung Galaxy Ace Duos—and dozens of late-night bootloops, hard bricks, and agonizing trial-and-error that tested my patience to its absolute limit. When everyone around me treated a smartphone as just a consumer toy, I saw a locked puzzle waiting to be reverse-engineered.

Winning Rank 1 (Class Topper) in the 14th SOF National Cyber Olympiad gave me undeniable proof that my obsession wasn't a distraction—it was my calling. For the next three straight years, I dedicated weekends and nights to study C, C++, Java, Arduino, Ruby, and Python under a teacher who understood my curiosity instead of taming it. When I moved to Pune University (SPPU), that hunger expanded across operating systems, database internals, low-level hardware programming, and music production. I have an insatiable, keen interest in how everything works: from raw silicon registers and Sub-GHz radio waves to logistics for high-security government institutions, and modern distributed data lakehouses.

Most people stop when a system simply works; I only get interested when I understand why it works and how it breaks. When a network connection drops, it is not an abstract mystery—it's packets, socket buffers, and RF signal attenuation. When an OS throttles, it is governed by ACPI EC registers and thermal governors. My struggles taught me grit; my achievements proved that depth beats superficiality every single time. My never-ending curiosity is not a hobby—it is the single driving engine of my life.
